# Poker competitor research plan

Date: 2026-10-10
POC: Project owner
TL;DR: Compare decentralized poker protocols and crypto-funded poker rooms without treating crypto payments as proof of decentralization.

## Questions

- Which products have evidence of player-controlled cards, verifiable dealing, noncustodial funds, and on-chain settlement?
- What do players pay in rake, tournament entry charges, withdrawals, network gas, and token-related costs?
- Which privacy properties cover identity, hidden cards, public wallet activity, operators, and other players?
- Which products have documented current availability, and which are historical or have unresolved deployment status?
- What can Private Poker credibly claim today, and which competitive claims require further testing or implementation?

## Evidence strategy

A delegated research track will discover candidates and read primary documentation, fee pages, terms, security descriptions, and implementation repositories. Prefer explicit fee schedules to affiliate reviews. Record each source's publication date where available and distinguish the review date from the product's release or deployment date.

Check Glasser's catalog and pricing without running paid endpoints. Use existing free research facilities where they supply the same evidence. Paid requests require separate cost approval.

## Deliverables

- A linked wiki index.
- Competitor matrix with fee and privacy qualifications.
- Technical privacy, fairness, and decentralization comparison.
- Product implications and evidence gaps.

## Completion

Completed on 2026-10-10. The linked index, competitor comparison, privacy/trust analysis, and product-priority pages are written. Review checked relative links, fee categories, cited evidence, historical status, and local product limitations. No paid Glasser endpoint was run.

## Boundaries

This is desk research, not a smart-contract audit, a live liquidity test, or legal advice. Production availability, security, and fee schedules must not be inferred from marketing alone. Local product context comes from the project notes and implementation documentation; the Vite directory is the requested documentation destination, not the canonical frontend.
